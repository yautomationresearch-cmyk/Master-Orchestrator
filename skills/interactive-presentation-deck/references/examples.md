# Examples

These examples show how to reuse the skill without copying one visual theme.

## Neuroscience lecture

Use a dark editorial shell, selective scientific artwork, a five-step map reveal, bounded image scenes for neuron/anatomy, a comparison for chemistry, a classroom process scene, takeaway, and sources. Use cyan/violet only as secondary emphasis; labels, numbering, and position must carry meaning if the colors are removed.

## JP Morgan documentary

Use a documentary timeline, archival image scenes, a source trail, and restrained chapter navigation. Prefer a timeline or evidence composition over a dashboard. Keep image crop and caption contracts explicit so historical photographs remain readable and attribution is visible.

## Business explainer

Use one clear claim per scene, a process reveal, a comparison, and a final decision rule. Use brand colors only through semantic tokens. Avoid assuming that branded colors are the only way to distinguish categories; use labels, order, and shape as well.

## Color-independent teaching example

For a four-part framework, use numbered rows with distinct labels and spacing. Add color as a secondary highlight, but keep the framework understandable in grayscale, with a screen reader, and when projected in poor lighting.
