# Evidence rules

Use evidence proportionate to the subject and the consequence of being wrong.

## Source routing

- scientific or medical claims: primary papers, government sources, universities, professional societies, maintained reference texts
- financial or business claims: filings, official reports, authoritative datasets, transparent methodology
- legal or regulatory claims: current primary law, regulation, or official guidance
- historical/documentary claims: primary archives plus reputable secondary scholarship
- product or software behavior: current official documentation

Browse when the fact may have changed, the user asks for citations, or the subject is niche or high-stakes. Do not invent precise numbers, quotations, URLs, or source labels.

## Claim hygiene

Separate visible claims into:

- measured finding
- interpretation or inference
- teaching metaphor
- open question or uncertainty

Do not present a metaphor as anatomy, a correlation as causation, or an association as a universal rule. If the evidence is mixed, write the uncertainty into the note or visible caveat rather than hiding it.

## Source placement

Use short source labels on evidence-bearing scenes and a readable research-trail scene at the end. Put long citations and methodology in notes or a linked source document when visible text would damage the composition.

## Asset provenance

Track generated artwork separately from researched claims. State when an image is AI-generated, and do not imply that generated visual detail is scientific evidence.
